Seed Phrase Safety: 7 Rules Every Crypto Beginner Should Know

When you create a crypto wallet, it gives you a seed phrase: a list of 12 or 24 ordinary words. Those words are the master key to everything in that wallet. Anyone who has them can take your crypto, and if you lose them, no company can recover your funds for you. That is why seed phrase safety matters more than almost anything else a beginner learns. These seven rules keep it simple.

1. Never share your seed phrase with anyone

No real support team, exchange, or wallet company will ever ask for your seed phrase. Anyone who asks for it — by email, chat, phone, or a website form — is trying to steal from you. There are no exceptions to this rule, and no legitimate reason for anyone else to need your words.

2. Write it on paper, never store it digitally

Write the words down by hand on paper, in the correct order, and check them twice. Do not take a photo of them, do not save them in a notes app, a cloud drive, or an email to yourself. Anything stored on a phone or computer can be copied by malware, a hacked account, or a stolen device. Paper cannot be hacked.

3. Keep two copies in separate safe places

One copy can be lost to fire, water, or a move. Make a second handwritten copy and keep it somewhere else safe — for example, one at home and one with a trusted family member or in a safe deposit box. Both copies should be out of sight and protected from damage.

4. Never type it into a website or app that asks

Fake wallet websites and apps are one of the most common crypto scams. They look real, but their only job is to collect the seed phrase you type in. Only ever enter your seed phrase into the official wallet app you installed yourself, and only when you are restoring a wallet you already own.

5. Test your backup before you trust it

Before you put a meaningful amount of crypto in a wallet, practise restoring it. Delete the wallet app from a spare device, restore it using only your written seed phrase, and check that the same wallet appears. If the restore works, your backup is good. If it does not, you have found the problem while it is still cheap to fix.

6. Use a hardware wallet for larger amounts

A hardware wallet is a small physical device that keeps your keys offline. Even if your computer is infected, the keys never leave the device. It does not replace the seed phrase rules above — you still need your written backup — but it removes the biggest everyday risk, which is a compromised computer or phone.

7. If your seed phrase is ever exposed, move your funds fast

If you suspect anyone has seen your words — you typed them into a suspicious site, someone photographed them, or a copy went missing — treat the wallet as compromised. Create a brand-new wallet with a brand-new seed phrase, and move all your crypto to it immediately. Do not wait to see if anything happens; by then it is usually too late.
